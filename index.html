Why it opens on your favourites

You don't use 420 effects.
You use the same few.

One editor, three months of real work: 1,279 effects and presets applied, 41 different items out of about 420 in the palette. Three of them made up 74%. So Khanjar opens on what you use, and ⌘1 applies your number one without typing.

What it finds

Your presets,
wherever they come from.

  • Yours, bought, or from a friend. Khanjar reads the presets in your own Premiere: the ones you made, the packs you bought, the ones someone sent you. Adobe's presets are there too.
  • In any language. Presets saved in a Premiere set to another language work as well.
  • Animated presets keep their timing. Keyframes are stretched to the clip or pinned to its start or end, the way Premiere does it, and eased curves are rebuilt frame by frame so the motion matches.
  • Duos. Two presets you always chain on the same clip become one line in the palette, applied in one go.
  • Fast. An effect is applied in about 5 to 15 ms; a preset on 283 clips took a third of a second. Khanjar does nothing until you press a shortcut.

Honest limits

They come from what Adobe's plugin API allows today.

  • Presets built on a mask, or on Lumetri curves, color wheels or LUTs, are left out rather than applied wrong. Lumetri presets that only move sliders work.
  • A preset is marked partial when one of its effects or settings can't be set through the API. The rest is applied.
  • Eased animations are rebuilt frame by frame: the motion matches, but Effect Controls shows one keyframe per frame.
  • It can't create an adjustment layer. Make one once in your project, Khanjar reuses it.
  • Audio effects and transitions aren't supported yet.

Install

Up and running
in 3 minutes.

  1. Download

    Get Khanjar-<version>.zip from the latest release on GitHub, unzip it and drag Khanjar.app into Applications.

  2. Allow the first launch

    The beta is not signed by Apple yet. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You do it once.

  3. Let it set itself up

    A magic-wand icon appears in the menu bar and a welcome window opens. Click Allow when macOS asks about your Documents folder, where your presets live. Khanjar installs its small plugin in Premiere through the Creative Cloud app.

  4. Press ⌘J in Premiere

    Select clips, press ⌘J, type a few letters, press Enter.

Khanjar is ready

  • Khanjar lives in your menu bar — the wand icon at the top right. It runs in the background.
  • In Premiere Pro, press ⌘J to open the palette. Search for an effect or a preset, then press ↵ to apply it to the selected clips.
  • You can also assign your favorite presets to keyboard shortcuts in Settings.
  • On first launch, macOS may ask for access to your Documents folder (to read your presets) — click “Allow”.

FAQ

Questions
editors ask.

Is there a command palette for Adobe Premiere Pro?

Yes, that is what Khanjar is: press ⌘J in Premiere, type a few letters of any effect or preset, press Enter. It is free, for Mac.

How do I apply a preset to many clips at once?

Select the clips in the timeline, press ⌘J, pick the preset, press Enter. It lands on every selected clip in one step; linked audio is skipped, like in Premiere. Undo takes one ⌘Z for an effect, two or three for a preset.

Can I give a preset its own keyboard shortcut?

Yes: menu bar icon › Settings… › Add a shortcut…, pick the preset, press the keys. It works whenever Premiere is in front.

Does it work with the preset packs I bought?

Yes. Khanjar reads your Premiere Effect Presets, where installed packs live, and Adobe's own presets, even presets saved in a Premiere set to another language. Third-party effects used by a pack must be installed.

Which Premiere versions? Windows? Intel Macs?

Premiere Pro 2026 (26.x) and later, tested on 26.5, on a Mac with Apple Silicon and macOS 13 or later. No Windows and no Intel Macs for now.

Does it slow Premiere down?

No. Khanjar does nothing until you press a shortcut. An effect is applied in about 5 to 15 ms; a preset on 283 clips took a third of a second.

Does it send anything online?

Khanjar runs on your Mac and talks to Premiere over a local connection that web pages can't use. Your clips, projects and presets never leave your computer. The only thing that uses the internet is the optional update check (privacy details).

Is it free?

Free and open source, under the GPL-3.0 license. It is a beta, made by Isma, a video editor, with Claude Code. Not affiliated with Adobe.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit Khanjar, move it to the Trash and remove its plugin with one command: see Uninstall in the guide.
